## 2. Risk read

- **Distinctiveness in Arabic is the biggest risk.** وضّاح (root و-ض-ح,
  "clear, bright"; "bright-faced") can read as laudatory or descriptive for
  appearance services in Arabic-speaking offices (KSA, UAE, Kuwait). Get a
  registrability opinion before paying international fees. **[confirm with counsel]**
- **Benelux / EU:** WADDAH is less exposed. Examiners look at how the relevant
  public understands the mark; an Arabic meaning rarely counts there, but it is
  not ruled out.
- **Common first name:** some GCC laws limit registering another person's name
  without consent. Low risk for a generic first name; ask the agent.
- **Non-use:** the B.V. will trade in the GCC, not the Benelux. A Benelux mark
  unused for 5 years can be revoked, and during the first 5 years losing the
  base mark also cancels the Gulf designations (central attack). Keep proof of
  use in every country (dated ads, invoices, local-currency pricing pages).

## 5. Filing plan [confirm with counsel]

1. **WADDAH (Latin):** Benelux word mark in classes **44, 41, 42** (add 9 only
   if a native app is built), with narrow 9/42 wording such as "software for
   aesthetic face and body analysis".
2. **Madrid application within 6 months** of the Benelux filing (Paris
   Convention art. 4C(1) priority), designating the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain and
   Oman, plus Saudi Arabia once its membership is confirmed (otherwise a
   national Saudi filing). Madrid can be filed straight after the Benelux
   filing, without waiting for registration (Benelux opposition window about 2
   months from publication; confirm with BOIP).
3. **وضّاح (Arabic):** EUIPO accepts only Latin, Greek or Cyrillic word marks,
   and BOIP very likely does the same (unconfirmed). Choose between:
   (a) **national word-mark filings** in each GCC office, which accept Arabic
   script; or (b) a **figurative Benelux base** through Madrid, which gives
   narrower, styling-bound protection. Get quotes for both. A Madrid
   application for an Arabic mark must give a Latin transliteration ("WADDAH");
   agree any translation ("bright/radiant") with counsel, because it can feed
   the descriptiveness objection.
4. **Kuwait:** national filings for both forms. Start consular legalisation of
   the power of attorney now; Arabic transliteration is required.
5. **Later:** EU mark only if trading in the EU; the logo once final, with a
   written copyright assignment from the designer; Egypt, Morocco and Tunisia
   by later designation; Jordan, Iraq and Lebanon nationally.
6. **Budget** for provisional-refusal responses through local agents,
   especially for the Arabic mark (refusal periods 12 months, or 18 where a
   country has declared it).
7. **Timing:** file before the public launch and before Meta ad spend; the
   GCC systems are first-to-file in practice. Use "™", not "®", until
   registered in a given country.
