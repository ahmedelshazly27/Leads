## Hook formula

1. Start with 10–30 s of music and images: an establishing wide, a teaser of a later moment, sometimes with song lyrics in the captions. Examples: `5qBvCB1eejI@0:00–0:30`, `V7Nzx6u94ug@0:00–0:20`, `eVVfWFlvF14@0:00–0:14`, `lR7uALsCdoo@0:00–0:49`.
2. The teaser images often come back later as the payoff. The snorkel at `5qBvCB1eejI@0:10` returns at `@31:04`. The palm-pay at `lR7uALsCdoo@0:20` returns at `@11:02`.
3. Then a casual spoken premise ("so I'm currently down in Australia…", "good morning, bro"). First speech arrives at 1.6–14 s.
4. The title does the selling (age, entrepreneur, place). The openings rarely state a stake or a question.
5. Exception: `9UK-p6Ea7vI` is the most cinematic opening. It cuts landscape awe into a borrowed philosophical voiceover over b-roll of local life (`@0:00–1:29`).

## Structure & pacing

- Chronological multi-day diary. Days are marked by the yellow cards, not by narration. YouTube chapters are descriptive (5 of 6 videos have them).
- The rhythm alternates: a work beat (calls, laptop, business update), then a leisure or travel beat (gym, boat, run, food), roughly every 1–4 minutes.
- Pacing is slow overall. Estimated cuts per minute are 4.2–10.4, and the median visual sequence is 50–110 s. The first 60 s are busier (an estimated 18 cuts/min in 5 of 6 videos), and then long static blocks drop the rate to near zero.
- One human set piece per film: auntie (`XEYCEV_EdnY@6:58–16:25`), meetups, grandma dinner (`eVVfWFlvF14@12:53–14:12`).
- The ending is a reflection monologue. Sometimes it is a cliff-hanger into the next episode instead (`eVVfWFlvF14@27:05`).

## Camera & composition

- The working camera is a wide-angle handheld held at arm's length or below the face. Faces sit big in frame, and the background shows the place.
- For the long talks, the camera is locked off at mid height on a table or stool. The creator is seated, gestures with his hands, and holds a notebook or drink (`V7Nzx6u94ug@1:00`, `eVVfWFlvF14@8:25`, `XEYCEV_EdnY@28:52`).
- Inserts: hands, passport, phone screens, laptop screens (`lR7uALsCdoo@8:14`, `5qBvCB1eejI@18:21`, `V7Nzx6u94ug@6:17`).
- Observational wides cover crowds and human scenes (`lR7uALsCdoo@18:47`, `XEYCEV_EdnY@8:57`).
- Aspect ratio is sometimes inconsistent. Pillarboxed footage appears in `XEYCEV_EdnY@1:10–2:39` and `@16:45–18:15`, and phone stories are placed vertically on black.
- Exact cuts, transitions and camera moves are not observable at storyboard resolution. Motion-blurred frames suggest whip or zoom moves: `XEYCEV_EdnY@8:18`, `5qBvCB1eejI@14:53`, `lR7uALsCdoo@10:33`.

## Colour & grade

- Warm bias in every video (profile warmth 0.056–0.143). Saturation is moderate (0.34–0.50) and contrast mild (0.14–0.23).
- Daylight is left natural, and windows are allowed to blow out (`5qBvCB1eejI@7:56`, `V7Nzx6u94ug@14:13`).
- Nights and interiors use practical light: amber lamps (`V7Nzx6u94ug@9:55`), tungsten bars (`9UK-p6Ea7vI@7:52`), a red booth (`9UK-p6Ea7vI@11:19`), a sodium-orange street (`lR7uALsCdoo@18:47`).
- Sunsets are used as silhouette moments (`5qBvCB1eejI@27:16`, `@0:30`).

## Sound & narration

- Talk-dominant. Speech covers 74–98% of the runtime at 106–176 wpm. Almost none of it is scripted voiceover. It is live talk to camera plus overheard conversation.
- Music works as a bed under the opening montage, with song lyrics often captioned (`5qBvCB1eejI`, `V7Nzx6u94ug`, `lR7uALsCdoo`). It returns in short 13–35 s speech-free beats at emotional or arrival points: `XEYCEV_EdnY` auntie scenes, `lR7uALsCdoo@8:59` entering the conference, `9UK-p6Ea7vI@10:40` night garden.
- Laughter and banter are left in, with expletives censored in the captions.

## When this approach fits a scene

- You have a lot of footage of yourself talking. A wide lens at arm's length, a locked-off sit-down, and talk in cars and transit all fit.
- Days blur together and need orientation. Yellow time/place cards do that cheaply.
- There is a warm encounter with a person (a relative, a local, fans). Let it run long, observational, with translated captions and music instead of narration.
- Arrivals and border crossings: an insert (passport, signage), a bold place card, then a selfie reaction.
- An end-of-trip reflection where the creator's voice is the point.
- It fits less well for landscape-led, music-driven montage films. Only `9UK-p6Ea7vI` leans that way.

## What NOT to copy

- Multi-minute static monologues with no cutaways. In a travel film, cut them down or cover them with b-roll.
- Business or revenue talk, email-list and product calls to action, and screen-recorded tools. These serve his personal brand, not the trip.
- Borrowed voiceover from someone else's lecture (`9UK-p6Ea7vI@0:20`), because of rights and authenticity.
- Mixed aspect ratios left unmanaged, and faces blown out against windows.
- His exact yellow-card look and "pov: 27 y/o entrepreneur" title template. Adapt the idea (a bold place/time card), not the brand.
