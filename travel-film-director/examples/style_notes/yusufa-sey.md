## Hook formula

1. **0-20 s: a visual lead-in without context.** It is one of the following:
   - a cold open inside the action (car and campaign at `urdgXDBc_So@0:00`)
   - a teaser montage with a stakes overlay (`Z6mhiK7E3Sk@0:10` "242 DAYS LEFT")
   - a music-led stat overlay (`dUL-YXeWDQY@0:00` revenue counter over a rap track)
   - mood details (`NOU9AVeHjhI@0:00`)
   - an arrival (`IfAA5Hy-8Zw@0:10`)
   - a greeting piece to camera (`XC-Nls8KhCI@0:00`)
2. **About 0:20-0:40: the series card on black** (where used).
3. **Immediately after: a piece to camera** that states where he is and what has to happen ("we're supposed to close today", `dUL-YXeWDQY@1:29`; "Meetings. How you feel?", `NOU9AVeHjhI@0:15`).

The profiles mark only `dUL-YXeWDQY` as a spoken cold open. Elsewhere the pattern is "short visual lead-in followed by speech". The hooks rely on access and status (politicians, footballers, yachts, Parliament) more than on a question or a cliffhanger.

## Structure & pacing

- **Chronological, chaptered (6-10 YouTube chapters per video), day-in-the-life.** Business is woven between travel and leisure. Time jumps are marked with text cards, not montage.
- **Long held scenes.** Median visual-sequence length is 49-89 s. Some sit-downs run 3+ minutes in one setup (`urdgXDBc_So@12:10`–`14:58`, `IfAA5Hy-8Zw@6:06`–`9:04`). NOU9AVeHjhI is almost entirely interview, with four reframed setups.
- **Estimated cuts per minute range from 4.3 (dUL-YXeWDQY) to 9.2 (IfAA5Hy-8Zw).** These are statistical estimates from storyboard samples. The pace is conversational, not montage-driven.
- **Breathers.** Short speech-free montages give relief (yacht wake at `IfAA5Hy-8Zw@14:40`, monkey park at `Z6mhiK7E3Sk@9:53`, match at `Z6mhiK7E3Sk@14:10`).
- **Payoff at the end.** The strongest arcs save the emotional or business payoff for the last third: deal completion (`dUL-YXeWDQY@16:31`), the candidate's in-car reflection (`urdgXDBc_So@15:28`), and the trophy and farewell (`Z6mhiK7E3Sk@16:28`, `@19:07`).

## When this approach fits a scene

- **Access and relationship stories** (following a politician, hosting a guest, closing a deal): observational table scenes plus car ride-alongs plus one stakes-setting piece to camera.
- **Multi-day trips:** black "THE NEXT DAY" cards or time-of-day labels instead of transition montages.
- **Explaining abstract business or ideas:** a seated interview with several reframed setups, a whiteboard, or a walk-and-talk.
- **Milestone moments:** film the real reaction in place and save it for the last third.
- **Not a fit** for scenery-first travel films: landscape b-roll here is sparse and only used as punctuation.

## What NOT to copy

- **Series branding:**
  - the "ZERO TO £100M" card
  - "VLOG nn / CURRENT REVENUE" counters
  - "NEXT RELEASE dd/mm/yyyy" and "SEE YOU NEXT TIME" cards
  - the "journey to 100 million" framing

  Build your own title system.
- **Appended product demos or community pitches inside the film** (`dUL-YXeWDQY@11:53`, `@19:29`–`29:43`).
- **Licensed music in a cold open, and heavy profanity**, if the edit is for broad or brand use.
- **Static 3-minute holds** without the speaker or access to carry them.
- **Titles that don't match the location on screen** (XC-Nls8KhCI: "UK" title, Copenhagen footage).
- **Blown-out magenta windows and crushed faces**, unless you want that look on purpose.
