## 1. YouTube channel (QOVES Studio): size, formats, cadence, funnel

### Takeaway
QOVES Studio (@qovesstudio) has about 1M subscribers, about 330M lifetime views and about 700 videos (May 2026). Growth is slow but steady, about +93K subscribers from Jul 2025 to May 2026. ## 4. Positioning and claims (accuracy, science, AI, experts, risky claims)

### Takeaway
Qoves positions itself as a science- and AI-driven "facial aesthetics" diagnostic company, explicitly non-surgical ("Improve Your Looks Without Surgery"), with human specialist review. It leans on precise-sounding metrics: 521 landmarks, 160+ or 200+ tests, 18 facial sections, "2000+ academic studies". It also uses a branded morph engine (Remorph) for "after" visualizations, while disclaiming licensed medical services. The riskiest areas for regulators are the morph "after" images, quantified attractiveness scores ("49/100 of potential"), surgical or filler recommendations (including a paid surgical add-on), and reach to teens.

### Cited Findings
- Homepage title: "QOVES - Improve Your Looks Without Surgery" — [qoves.com (search result)](https://www.qoves.com/)
- Self-description: "state-of-the-art landmarking technologies" mapping 521 facial points; scores for traits such as facial masculinity and symmetry; "more than 160 beauty tests" — via search summary of qoves.com, in [Uncynical](https://uncynical.substack.com/p/i-tried-qoves-which-isnt-a-looksmaxxing) / [qoves.com](https://www.qoves.com/)
- Comparison pages claim a specialist reviews every analysis, "18 facial sections, more than 200 tests," written report, $150 a year — [qoves.com vs LooksMax AI](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-looksmax-ai)
- Trustpilot business description: "a leading company in the field of biotechnologies applied to facial aesthetics… groundbreaking accuracy in facial diagnostics… realistic facial visualizations using the Qoves Remorph engine" — [Trustpilot](https://fi.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com)
- Wikipedia: "American biotechnology company" in Delaware building "facial diagnostic tools using computer vision and artificial intelligence"; Vogue calls it a "facial aesthetics consulting" company; $750K venture funding from Share Ventures (2024); founders Shafee Hassan (engineer and anthropologist researching attractiveness with computer vision since 2019) and Leo Olsen Guillot. Wikipedia is internally inconsistent (2019 vs Feb 2024 incorporation) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qoves)

- Same reviewer: the product "uses AI to make [your photos] look like a better looking version of you"; suggestions ranged from retinol and sunscreen to cheek filler — [Uncynical](https://uncynical.substack.com/p/i-tried-qoves-which-isnt-a-looksmaxxing)
- A creator reports being rated "49/100 of their potential" — [YouTube review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BNKqMzlSXD4)
- Pricing and offer (2026): about $150 base, often described as $150 a year for one reviewed analysis; about 28-day delivery with an $80 express add-on; $180 optional surgical-recommendations upgrade (reportedly cited by the Guardian; the article itself was not found). Before about mid-2026, separate one-time products at about $160–$435 were reported. These are competitor or affiliate sources, so treat as indicative — [softmaxx.io pricing](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing), [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review), [Medium review](https://meetdavidalex.medium.com/qoves-full-review-bf17999832fd)

- Criticism: the morph "after" image is the most complained-about element ("looks like a different person"); analysis "leans toward clinical procedures"; claims of reach to teenagers with psychologists' concern (single opinion piece, unverified) — [Medium, Best Looksmaxing Apps 2026](https://medium.com/@lennarthewriter/best-looksmaxing-apps-of-2026-28ee36c0d156)

- No regulatory action against Qoves was found — [search across sources above]

### Inferences
- Claim-risk map for a compliant Gulf competitor:
  - AI-generated "after" morphs work like before/after imagery, which ad policies and UAE/KSA advertising and health rules tend to scrutinize.
  - "Biotechnology" and "diagnostic" language implies a medical function, despite the non-clinical disclaimer.
  - Numeric attractiveness scores and "% of potential" can feed negative self-perception.
  - The surgical-recommendation upsell from a non-licensed provider is risky.
  - Precise-sounding but unvalidated accuracy claims (521 points, "groundbreaking accuracy") are risky without published validation.
- Positioning gaps to exploit: published validation or peer-reviewed methodology; licensed clinician involvement; regional, ethnicity-aware norms (Qoves already makes ethnicity-specific content, e.g. Malaysian men); no-score or wellbeing-safe framing; Arabic-language content.

### Gaps
- Exact current homepage copy, guarantees and refund terms were not verified (site blocked).
- The Guardian and Vogue articles were not found directly.
- No published accuracy validation by Qoves was found.

## 5. Brand voice and visual identity

### Takeaway
The voice is clinical, academic and blunt ("anthropology and cognitive psychology", "facial diagnostics", "tests", "sections"), mixed with a soft glow-up framing ("Improve Your Looks Without Surgery", "Glow Up In Your Twenties"). The brand distances itself from the "looksmaxxing" label while its audience and forum presence sit squarely in that community.

### Cited Findings
- Customers describe the report as "blunt, clinical feedback" — [Trustpilot](https://it.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com)
- Branding uses the all-caps wordmark "QOVES", the "Studio" suffix for the media arm, and trademarked tech naming ("Remorph™") — [Trustpilot](https://fi.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com), [Favikon](https://app.favikon.com/public/profile/qoves-studio-63961b8f2785853b94ed23a8/)
- The report is titled "QOVES STUDIO AESTHETICS REPORT … / COMPREHENSIVE EDITION", an editorial, dossier-like format — [looksmax.org PDF](https://i.looksmax.org/attachments/2025/05/4928024_Angel_Olavarria_Comprehensive_Report_1.pdf)
- Asks users not to call it looksmaxxing — [Uncynical](https://uncynical.substack.com/p/i-tried-qoves-which-isnt-a-looksmaxxing)
- Glow-up language in TikTok and podcast titles ("Science-Based Advice to Max Out Your Looks") — [Chartable](https://chartable.com/podcasts/qoves-glow-up-science-based-advice-to-max-out-your-looks), [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/qovesstudio?lang=en)

### Inferences
- The brand has a dual voice: research lab for credibility, glow-up coach for conversion. "Max out your looks" echoes looksmaxxing vocabulary, which conflicts with the stated distancing.
