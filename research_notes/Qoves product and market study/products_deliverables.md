# Qoves (qoves.com / "QOVES Studio"): Product Range, Pricing and Deliverables

Research date: 2026-11-08. **Method caveat:** in this session, direct fetches of qoves.com, web.archive.org and trustpilot.com were blocked by the network. Every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the pages in full. Many secondary sources are competitor blogs (SoftMaxx, Facet, TheFaceAnalysis, TheFaceReport, RealWorldAppeal) with a commercial interest, so they are labelled where they are used. Before relying on any figure, check it against the live qoves.com checkout, the /terms page and Wayback snapshots.

## 1. Product and service catalogue: current and historical, with prices

### Takeaway
As of late 2026, Qoves sells one core product: the **QOVES Membership at $150/year**, which covers one human-reviewed facial analysis plus a personalised protocol. Paid add-ons are reported at Express Delivery $80, Surgical Recommendations $180 and Hairstyle Suggestions $25. Until roughly mid-2026 it sold a catalogue of one-off PDF reports priced from about $100 to $435.

### Cited Findings
**Current (2026)**
- Qoves' own comparison page describes a yearly membership at $150/year, cancel any time. Per the page, it covers the analysis, a personalised protocol, biometric scores, visualisations of non-surgical change, and messaging with the "care team". — [qoves.com: QOVES vs Umax](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax)
- One source reports that the qoves.com pricing card still showed "Qoves Membership", "One analysis per year", $150, on 25 Sep 2026. — [SoftMaxx: QOVES Price 2026 (competitor)](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing)
- One page lists the plan at €150 in Europe, which suggests localised currency. — [SoftMaxx pricing (competitor)](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing)
- Reported add-ons: Express Delivery (24–48h) $80, Surgical Recommendations $180, Hairstyle Suggestions $25. These do not appear in the homepage pricing block, and one source estimates a realistic spend of $150–$410. — [SoftMaxx: QOVES Studio explained](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-studio); [RealWorldAppeal review](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-review)
- The $180 surgical upgrade and $80 express option were reportedly found by The Guardian (Aug 2026). The article text was not accessed. — [SoftMaxx pricing (competitor), citing the Guardian](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing)
- Pricing data is ambiguous: the FAQ reportedly calls it a "yearly subscription", while the structured pricing data labels it "one-time". — [SoftMaxx/TheFaceAnalysis via search summary](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- One competitor says the price is shown only at checkout and the site is rendered by JavaScript, which explains why third-party price summaries disagree. — [TheFaceReport (competitor)](https://thefacereport.com/guides/qoves-alternative)

**Historical one-off reports (v1.qoves.com, roughly 2023–2024 pages; retired around mid-2026)**
- Preliminary Aesthetics Report: $150, about 10 pages, "a quick assessment of the face as a whole". — [v1.qoves.com Preliminary](https://v1.qoves.com/product/preliminary-aesthetics-report/)
- Tertiary Aesthetics Report: $250, 16 pages, 32 measurements. — [v1.qoves.com Tertiary](https://v1.qoves.com/product/tertiary-aesthetics-report/)
- Comprehensive Aesthetics Report: $300, 24 pages, 38 measurements of the front and sides of the face. — [v1.qoves.com Comprehensive](https://v1.qoves.com/product/comprehensive-aesthetics-report/)
- Hairline Design Report: $250, a 24-page "photogrammetric" assessment of the hair and hairline for planning surgical and non-surgical hair-loss treatment. — [v1.qoves.com Hairline](https://v1.qoves.com/product/hairline-report/)
- Facial Morph Report: $100. — [v1.qoves.com Facial Morph](https://v1.qoves.com/product/facial-morph/)
- Style Lookbook: "fashion and styling advice for your face & body". No price was found. — [SoftMaxx: QOVES Studio explained](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-studio)
- The catalogue of separate reports (analysis, hairline design, style lookbook, morphs) ran at about $160–$435 until mid-2026 and is now gone. — [SoftMaxx: QOVES Studio explained (competitor)](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-studio)
- Reviewer-quoted prices: $300 (July 2023 YouTube video, "I Paid $300…"); $435 for a bundle (July 2026); a Trustpilot UK reviewer paid about £150. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AMiE3_UavZk); [SoftMaxx pricing](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing); [Trustpilot CA](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com)
- The earliest product (about 2020–21) was a free online "Facial Assessment Tool", an AI that rated attractiveness and listed "flaws" such as under-eye bags and smile lines. — [MIT Technology Review, 5 Mar 2021](https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/03/05/1020133/ai-algorithm-rate-beauty-score-attractive-face/amp/)
- Directory-sourced tiers ($35 basic / $149 comprehensive / $300) are flagged by the aggregator itself as possibly out of date. — [Tenere Team aggregator](https://qoves.tenereteam.com/)

**Apps**
- No official Qoves App Store or Google Play listing was found. An AppGoblin page titled "Qutie - Become Attractive" mentions "Quoves" with $4.99/week and $9.99/month pricing. It is probably an unrelated or knock-off app and is unverified. — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6786037634)

**Free content / funnel**
- A YouTube channel (@qovesstudio) with about 1.05M subscribers (vidIQ, 26 Oct 2026; HypeAuditor reports about 1,055,888 in early Nov 2026; Playboard showed 925K at an earlier date). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@qovesstudio/); [HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/youtube/UCnlvhYDQLq4d_C3JYEVnWAQ/); [Playboard](https://playboard.co/en/channel/UCnlvhYDQLq4d_C3JYEVnWAQ)
- A large SEO "insights" hub of comparison pages (vs Umax, LooksMax AI, Haut.AI, YouCam, Perfect Corp, Face++, Amazon Rekognition). — [qoves.com insights examples](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-perfect-corp)

### Inferences
- Qoves has moved from tiered one-off PDF reports (10/16/24 pages) to a single membership with upsells. The headline price fell (from $300 to $150), and revenue now comes from add-ons and renewals.
- To match it, a competitor needs a $150-class anchor product with clear add-ons for speed, surgical options and hair/style.

### Gaps
- The exact current checkout price list, renewal mechanics and regional prices could not be verified on qoves.com (fetch blocked).
- No Wayback snapshots could be read, so the dated price history is incomplete.
- No price was found for the Style Lookbook, and no facial-hair or makeup product was found. No body analysis product or course was found.

## 2. Deliverable contents: format, pages, sections, visuals

### Takeaway
The historical deliverable was a long-form, academic-styled PDF "Facial Aesthetics Report" of 10, 16 or 24 pages, organised by anatomical region with references and an appendix. The 2026 membership is marketed as 18 sections and 200+ tests, plus a protocol with timeline and budget, before-and-after "morph" visualisations and biometric scores.

### Cited Findings
- Current marketing says specialists review 18 facial sections with 200+ aesthetic tests, then deliver a written report and an improvement plan. A product image on the same site refers to 47 analysis modules and 1,200+ measurements across nine photos. Note that sources elsewhere say six photos are required. — [qoves.com: QOVES vs Perfect Corp](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-perfect-corp); [qoves.com: QOVES vs Umax](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax)
- Per Qoves' own pages, the protocol ends in a projected result, a timeline and an estimated cost, with an itemised budget showing monthly cost and expected return. — [qoves.com insights (via search summary)](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax)
- Sample "QOVES Studio Facial Aesthetics Report" PDFs (Comprehensive, including a 2022 sample and a "V2") have a table of contents covering cephalometric variability, orbital/periocular features, chin and lip profile, facial proportions, facial symmetry, dentofacial assessment, midface, jaw/side profile, recommendations, references and an appendix. — [Scribd sample 1](https://www.scribd.com/document/554829284/QOVES-Sample-1-Comprehensive-Report); [Scribd sample V2](https://www.scribd.com/document/683724839/Sample-FINAL-Comprehensive-Report-V2); [Studocu customer report](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/zirve-universitesi/steel-framing/angel-olavarria-comprehensive-report/100551583)
- One reviewer describes a "facial assessments" section followed by an "analysis" section. — [Medium: Alessa Weiler review](https://alessaweiler.medium.com/my-honest-qoves-review-i-tried-their-aesthetic-analysis-these-are-my-results-be7aa6d9b61f)
- A sponsored reviewer counted 16 pages covering 11 features, and a Reddit reviewer said it took almost an hour to read. Seven of 11 reviewers discussed the before-and-after "morph" of their face. Protocols varied: some suggested filler or Botox, another was mostly makeup and skincare. — [RealWorldAppeal Reddit verdict (competitor)](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-reddit-verdict)
- Fans praise the depth and the diagrams, while critics call it generic or formulaic. — [RealWorldAppeal (competitor)](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-reddit-verdict)
- Earlier tool claims: maps 521 facial points and assesses 160+ beauty markers. Third-party listings instead cite 112 parameters and a 35,000+ image training set, so the figures conflict. — [search summary of secondary sources incl. remio.ai](https://www.remio.ai/post/qoves-facial-analysis-turns-beauty-scores-into-treatment-plans-but-measurement-i)
- Current positioning is non-surgical ("glow up without surgery"); surgical recommendations are a paid add-on. — [qoves.com insights](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax); [SoftMaxx](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-studio)

### Inferences
- The visual language is clinical and academic: anatomical terms such as cephalometric and dentofacial, measurement-based diagrams, cited references, and morph images. Matching the depth means region-by-region sections, measured landmarks, references, a costed and timed protocol, and a simulated "after" image.

### Gaps
- Exact current section names and order for the 18-section report were not found. It is also unconfirmed whether delivery is PDF or web dashboard, and whether reports show percentiles or numeric scores beyond "biometric scores".
- The Scribd samples could not be opened, so per-section visual details (annotated photos, chart types) are unverified.
- No YouTube report-walkthrough content was accessed.

## 3. Turnaround, inputs, revisions, follow-ups, calls

### Takeaway
Standard delivery is "up to 28 days" from six standardised photos plus a questionnaire. Express 24–48h delivery costs $80 extra. Messaging with a "care team" is included. No evidence was found of live video consultations or a formal revision policy.

### Cited Findings
- The analysis uses six photos and a questionnaire, with delivery within up to 28 days. Express 24–48h costs $80. — [SoftMaxx pricing (competitor)](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing); [RealWorldAppeal](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-review)
- One reviewer questions whether six standardised angles capture how a person looks in ordinary photos. — [RealWorldAppeal](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-review)
- The membership includes "messaging with the care team" and protocol tracking. — [qoves.com: QOVES vs Umax](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax)
- The long wait is a common complaint. — [RealWorldAppeal Reddit verdict](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-reddit-verdict)

### Inferences
- A 28-day turnaround suggests a manual or human-review queue. A faster turnaround is an obvious place for a competitor to differentiate.

### Gaps
- No revision or re-analysis policy, consultation-call offering, or named plastic-surgeon involvement was found.

## 4. Human vs automated; team credentials

### Takeaway
Qoves now markets itself explicitly as "specialist-reviewed", in contrast to instant AI apps. Its origins, however, are an AI beauty-scoring tool, and no named surgeons or verified credentials were found.

### Cited Findings
- Qoves positions itself as a professional service in which specialists check every result, and contrasts this with Umax's instant automated selfie score with "no human involved". — [qoves.com: QOVES vs Umax](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax)
- Qoves started in 2019 as a photo-retouching/airbrushing studio for modelling agencies and moved to a combination of AI-driven analysis and plastic surgery in 2020. — [MIT Technology Review, 2021](https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/03/05/1020133/ai-algorithm-rate-beauty-score-attractive-face/amp/)
- Wikipedia describes the founder, Shafee Hassan, as an engineer and anthropologist who has studied facial attractiveness with computer vision since 2019. — [Wikipedia: Qoves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qoves)
- The Guardian (5 Aug 2026), as relayed by a review site, reportedly found that a researcher whose study Qoves cited did not endorse its use for recommendations. It also found that age limits and self-reporting safeguards could be bypassed, and that Qoves did not respond to a request for comment. — [TheFaceAnalysis summary (competitor)](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review); [streamlinefeed mirror headline](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/paid-face-scans-sell-science-that-experts-say-cannot-exist)
- An AIAAIC incident record from March 2021 flags concerns about bias related to race, ethnicity and age, and about transparency. — [AIAAIC](https://www.aiaaic.org/aiaaic-repository/ai-algorithmic-and-automation-incidents/qoves-ai-beauty-scoring)

### Inferences
- The likely workflow is an automated landmark and measurement pipeline with human review and write-up. "Specialist" is not tied to any named or credentialed person in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No names or credentials of the reviewing "specialists" were found, and no evidence of licensed surgeons on staff.

## 5. Refunds, terms, company background

### Takeaway
Refunds are reportedly available only before photos or the questionnaire are submitted, and cancelling stops renewal without refunding the current year. The contracting entity is reportedly Qoves Inc. (Delaware). Founding date, headquarters and team size conflict across sources.

### Cited Findings
- Full refund only before photo or questionnaire submission, none afterwards. "Cancel anytime" stops the next renewal but does not refund the current year. One Trustpilot reviewer reportedly got a refund only after posting a review. — [SoftMaxx pricing (competitor)](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing); [TheFaceAnalysis (competitor)](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- Contracting entity is Qoves Inc., governed by Delaware law. — [TheFaceAnalysis via search summary](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- Wikipedia: a Delaware-based private "biotechnology" company founded by Shafee Hassan and Leo Olsen Guillot; incorporated February 2024; raised $750,000 in venture funding in 2024. The infobox says 2019, which conflicts with the text. — [Wikipedia: Qoves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qoves)
- Tracxn: based in London, founded 2022 by Shafee Hassan, 67 employees as of 30 Sep 2026. SoftMaxx: a London consultancy founded 2019, with the About page claiming 121 team members. AIAAIC and Wikipedia place it in or found it in Sydney. — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/qoves/__IXoB5vNMX--3r42qrgd9bI_w-zZGQBkEomttWdJOfd0); [SoftMaxx](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-studio); [AIAAIC](https://www.aiaaic.org/aiaaic-repository/ai-algorithmic-and-automation-incidents/qoves-ai-beauty-scoring)
- Vogue has described Qoves as a "facial aesthetics consulting" company. — [Wikipedia: Qoves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qoves)

### Inferences
- The most consistent picture: founded in 2019 in Sydney as a retouching studio, now operating from London with a US Delaware entity (incorporated 2024) for fundraising and terms.

### Gaps
- The qoves.com /terms and /refund pages were not read directly. The Guardian article was not read. The co-founder Leo Olsen Guillot was confirmed only by Wikipedia.
