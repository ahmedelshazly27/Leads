> **Method caveat (important for the report writer):** Every direct page fetch failed in this session. qoves.com, web.archive.org, trustpilot.com, apps.apple.com, medium.com, softmaxx.io and bloomberg.com were all blocked by the network allowlist or by the fetch tool. ## Website information architecture and visual design

### Takeaway
As of late 2026, qoves.com is a single-offer conversion site: "Improve Your Looks Without Surgery" / "Start Your Glow Up Without Surgery", sold as a $150/yr membership. The design is calm and clinical: teal/slate on white, Neue Montreal plus a mono accent font, soft gradients and rounded cards. Trust rests on self-reported user counts, a Trustpilot score, testimonial cards and long-form "science" content. No "as seen in" press bar was confirmed. The older v1 site (around 2024 and earlier) was a WooCommerce-style catalogue of tiered one-time PDF reports.

### Inferences
- The site's arc: 2020–2024 was a menu of PDF report tiers (morph, preliminary, tertiary, comprehensive, hairline, skin). In 2025–2026 it collapsed into a single membership with add-on upsells, plus a dashboard and chat. That simplifies the pricing UI but moves monetisation into post-click add-ons.
- The look (slate-teal, mono type, metallic wordmark, gradients) signals "lab/biotech" rather than "beauty". A competitor can match it, or set itself apart with warmer, more human visuals.
- The trust stack leans on self-reported numbers and Trustpilot rather than clinical credentials or press badges. Credentialed reviewers or a visible press bar would be an obvious competitive edge.

## Ordering and onboarding flow

### Takeaway
The flow runs pay first, then create an account, then fill in a questionnaire (demographics, lifestyle, goals, self-reported body-image screening) inside the account, then upload about 6 guided photos, then wait up to 28 days (or 24–48 h with the $80 express add-on). The refund window closes once photos or the questionnaire are submitted. Delays and refund friction are the dominant complaints.

### Cited Findings
- Sequence: after payment you create a Qoves account, then fill in the questionnaire and upload photos inside the account — [search summary of qoves.com](https://www.qoves.com/)
- Questionnaire scope: "demographics, lifestyle, and goals". The exact fields are not published — [qoves.com](https://www.qoves.com/)
- Safeguards: the service is restricted to adults and asks users to self-report body-image concerns. The Guardian notes that both checks can be circumvented — [Guardian via reprint](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/paid-face-scans-sell-science-that-experts-say-cannot-exist)
- Photos: the official copy says 6 clear photos at six specific angles of face and head — [qoves.com](https://www.qoves.com/). One reviewer listed front, right side, left side, three-quarter, smiling and top-of-head, which they counted as 8 shots — [Medium, David Alex](https://meetdavidalex.medium.com/qoves-full-review-bf17999832fd); another source also says 8 photos — [search summary](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/qoves-review). The count conflicts between sources.
- Photo guidance: daylight or natural light; avoid the iPhone front camera; no makeup; neutral lighting; specified angles — [Medium, David Alex](https://meetdavidalex.medium.com/qoves-full-review-bf17999832fd); [futurepedia](https://www.futurepedia.io/tool/qoves)
- Wait time: up to 28 days standard. The Qoves FAQ attributes this to the team preparing the analysis and protocol. Express delivery takes 24–48 h; one buyer got results in 2 days — [softmaxx.io pricing](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-pricing); [thefaceanalysis.com](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- Refund policy: terms updated 15 May 2026 reportedly allow a full refund only before photos or the questionnaire are submitted — [softmaxx.io review](https://softmaxx.io/blog/qoves-review)
- Delay complaints: one customer waited over a month and was disappointed. A paid express order was about 5 days late, which Qoves blamed on an order surge. Another reported a 2-week overrun, then an email promising to expedite, then no delivery — [au.trustpilot p3](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=3); [nz.trustpilot](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com); [trustpilot p5](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=5)
- Refund complaints: one customer was refused a refund despite contacting Qoves within 14 days. Another was refunded only after posting a public review — [au.trustpilot p3](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=3); [uk.trustpilot p8](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=8)
- Sign-up requires name and email to access the analysis and plans — [similarlabs / directory summary](https://similarlabs.com/p/qoves)

### Inferences
- Asking for payment before any value is shown, then waiting up to 28 days, is the biggest UX weakness. An instant preview (an on-device scan with a partial score) before checkout and a visible progress tracker would directly address the top complaints.
- The paid express add-on gives Qoves an incentive to keep the standard queue slow. A competitor could advertise guaranteed fast turnaround.
- The photo count in the copy (6) does not match what reviewers report (8), which hints that the capture guidance is unclear. A guided in-camera capture with live pose and lighting checks would be an improvement.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the questionnaire fields, the upload validation (rejection of blurry or poorly lit photos, re-upload requests), the payment methods (Stripe, PayPal, Apple Pay) or the content of confirmation emails.
- I found no YouTube walkthrough transcript. One video exists ("I Got My Face Analysed by QOVES… Is It Worth It?"), but its content was not retrievable — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NnHtukkxpPs)

## Report viewing experience

### Takeaway
In 2026 the report is delivered into a web dashboard with a built-in chat to the team. Contents are analysis, protocol, biometric scores, before/after visualisations and a letter from the team, with annual updates. Before about 2024, reports were long, designed PDFs (16–30 pages) organised by facial region.

### Cited Findings
- The dashboard holds the report, and members can open a chat there for concerns, questions or clarifications — [qoves.com](https://www.qoves.com/)
- Deliverable components: analysis, protocol, biometric scores, before-and-after visualisations, a letter from the team, and updates every year — [thefaceanalysis.com / search summary](https://thefaceanalysis.com/guides/qoves-review)
- Structure claims: 18 facial sections and 200+ aesthetic tests (Qoves insights pages). Other sources say 160+ assessments, 100+ tests, and 521 or 529 mapped landmarks, which are inconsistent — [qoves.com insights](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax); [directory summaries](https://www.aitoolhunt.io/tool/qoves)
- A reviewer describes the report as broken down by every element of the face, from shape to tone, with comparisons to the general-population average — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=10)
- Older PDF format: sample reports are hosted on Scribd ("QOVES Studio Facial Aesthetics Report – Sample 1 Comprehensive"; "Sample FINAL Tertiary Report V3") — [Scribd sample 1](https://www.scribd.com/document/554829284/QOVES-Sample-1-Comprehensive-Report); [Scribd tertiary](https://www.scribd.com/document/796803515/Sample-FINAL-Tertiary-Report-V3). Tier lengths were 16 pages (Tertiary) and 24 pages (Comprehensive) — [v1.qoves.com](https://v1.qoves.com/product/comprehensive-aesthetics-report/). The Guardian cites a 30-page report (with surgical upgrade, $330 total) that recommended five surgical procedures — [Guardian via reprint](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/paid-face-scans-sell-science-that-experts-say-cannot-exist)
- Third-party directories still describe delivery as downloadable PDFs — [aipapas](https://aipapas.com/tools/ai-beauty-and-cosmetics/qoves-facial-assessment-tool/)

### Inferences
- The move from PDF to dashboard plus chat makes sense for a recurring membership, since it keeps users coming back for annual updates and Q&A. Interactive elements beyond chat and before/after images are not documented.
- If the before/after visualisations are image-generated, Qoves itself has warned that general-purpose generators can alter unrelated facial traits — [Remio](https://www.remio.ai/post/qoves-facial-analysis-turns-beauty-scores-into-treatment-plans-but-measurement-i). Faithful, region-constrained morphs are therefore a quality bar a competitor could beat.

### Gaps
- I could not find dashboard screenshots, the navigation inside the report (tabs or sections), interactive charts or landmark overlays, or a mobile versus desktop layout.
- It is unclear whether a PDF export still exists in the 2026 dashboard.

## Mobile app (App Store / Google Play)

### Takeaway
There is no confirmed official Qoves app. An iOS listing named "Qoves: Facial Analysis" / "Qoves: Face Analysis & Glow Up" (id6791538114) is published by **APPAI LABS**. It appears to be a renamed "FaceIQ" app with a weekly or yearly subscription, and it may not be affiliated with Qoves. Android presence is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- iOS listing "Qoves: Facial Analysis" by APPAI LABS, requires iOS 17+. It turns "a quick 3D face scan" into personalised style and grooming coaching — [App Store (RS)](https://apps.apple.com/rs/app/qoves-facial-analysis/id6791538114)
- The same id, listed as "Qoves: Face Analysis & Glow Up", shows IAPs Qoves Pro Yearly $49.99 and Qoves Pro Weekly $9.99. Release notes mention "new name and fresh new app icon", which suggests it was previously "FaceIQ" — [App Store (BO)](https://apps.apple.com/bo/app/qoves-face-analysis-glow-up/id6791538114?l=en-GB)
- A separate App Store app, "Qutie - Become Attractive", mentions "Quoves" in its description. It has an estimated ~210 MAU and under $10K per month in revenue, all from IAP — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6786037634)
- Aggregators disagree on Android: one lists Web/iOS/Android, another an Android "Photo Editing" app with 6 downloads. Neither cites an official Play listing — [videosdk.live](https://www.videosdk.live/ai-apps/qoves); [theaidb](https://theaidb.com/apps/QOVES)
- Qoves positions itself against apps in "QOVES vs Umax: App vs Expert (2026)", framing its human-reviewed web service as superior to instant AI apps — [qoves.com insights](https://www.qoves.com/insights/qoves-vs-umax)

### Inferences
- Qoves' brand is being borrowed by third-party scan apps, and Qoves has no instant mobile product. That leaves an open gap for a credible, instant, on-device scan app that leads into an expert report.
- The APPAI LABS pricing ($9.99/week, $49.99/yr) reflects the typical looksmax-app paywall pattern. It is a useful price reference for a competitor's app tier.

## User praise and complaints about UX

### Takeaway
Sentiment is broadly positive on Trustpilot (about 4.7). Users praise the detail, the actionable tips and the responsive team. Complaints centre on late delivery, refund refusals, generic or "superficial" findings, a mismatch with the marketing, and price. Communities like Reddit and looksmax are split on value.

### Cited Findings
- The Trustpilot AI summary (533 reviews) says reviewers are "overwhelmingly" positive. Some note the report offers nothing new if you already know your features — [Trustpilot p10](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=10)
- Praise: "100% worth the price"; "easy to understand, with ways to improve"; a responsive team; "very comprehensive" — [nl.trustpilot](https://nl.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com); [Trustpilot p10](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=10)
- Criticism: a 1★ review called the findings superficial and vague. A 2★ review said the experience did not match the expectations the marketing set. Others found it "primitive or generic", with recommendations unsuited to their face shape or overdone. It suits people who can handle blunt, clinical feedback — [Trustpilot p7](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=7); [Trustpilot p10](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=10)
- Price perception: a UK reviewer cited about £150 — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=10)
- Delays and refunds: see the onboarding section above — [au.trustpilot p3](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=3); [uk.trustpilot p8](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/qoves.com?page=8)
- looksmax.org thread "QOVES facial analysis, worth it?" (about mid-2025): mixed views. Some said it might be worth about $150; others said it was "more for women"; one noted a newer version was coming — [looksmax.org](https://looksmax.org/threads/qoves-facial-analysis-worth-it.1456357/)
- Critical framing: the business model sells the premise that appearance can be decomposed, graded and improved — [Remio](https://www.remio.ai/post/qoves-facial-analysis-turns-beauty-scores-into-treatment-plans-but-measurement-i); the Guardian reported that experts say the underlying "science" cannot exist — [Guardian via reprint](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/paid-face-scans-sell-science-that-experts-say-cannot-exist)

### Inferences
- The UX levers that matter most to users are speed and turnaround reliability, the specificity of findings to the individual, honest refund terms, and expectation-setting in marketing. Polish of the visuals is not what users complain about.
- Ethical framing (body-image safeguards, an option to conclude "no change needed") is both a reputational risk for Qoves and a point a competitor could set itself apart on.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads specific to r/Qoves or r/looksmaxing were surfaced. The real Reddit sentiment is reported only second-hand by competitor blogs.
- No app-store reviews were retrievable.
