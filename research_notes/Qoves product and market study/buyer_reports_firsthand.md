# First-hand buyer accounts of Qoves reports (supplied by the user, Nov 2026)

Source: two first-hand customer reviews pasted by the project owner into the conversation (original platform not stated; likely Reddit/forum). Treated as primary customer testimony; not independently verified.

## Takeaway
Buyers value Qoves less for scores than for a long, human-written, feature-by-feature explanation of *how each feature and combination of features is perceived*, the discovery of subtle traits they never noticed (asymmetries, a birthmark), concrete styling fixes tied to those traits, and candour about what is realistic. The weakest element was the "after" projection (made one buyer look older). The service reads as a luxury product that can feel overpriced.

## Cited findings (buyer 1 — bought regular $150 report + surgical + hair add-ons)
- Report felt like learning about one's own features "through a biopsychological lens"; every feature examined and explained; lengthy if read fully.
- Explains combination effects: e.g. sculpted, outward cheekbones + soft chin → "more feminine sculpted look instead of masculine".
- Detected subtle brow asymmetry and slight chin asymmetry the buyer had never noticed, partly by comparison to an "ideal" chin.
- Gave a practical styling fix for the brow asymmetry: side part instead of middle part.
- Projection image was disliked: "they made me look older"; still judged worth it overall.
- Includes many recommendations incl. dermatology procedures (ongoing cost/maintenance).
- Intake: ~22–23 questions; photos front, 45°, 90°; asks ethnicity/ancestry (multi-ancestry allowed); asks aesthetic preferences/reference faces (e.g. a K-pop idol) and hair characteristics (e.g. naturally frizzy hair).
- Buyer infers features are compared against averages for people of the same background (e.g. a higher-than-average nose bridge for a Middle Eastern client would be explained in terms of profile and masculinity/femininity).
- Buyer believes reports are written by humans in the "QOVES Team" (typos, double spaces, wording), team composition unknown; debate exists about AI involvement.
- Value perception: "luxury service", "might feel overpriced", "set your standards low".

## Cited findings (buyer 2)
- "Better than I expected… really detailed"; picked up a birthmark the buyer was insecure about and gave specific advice.
- Hyperpigmentation recommendations were helpful.
- "Honest about what was realistic and what wasn't, which made me trust the report more."

## Inferences for a competitor
- Must match: per-feature narrative explanations in perception terms; combination/harmony commentary; subtle asymmetry detection with actionable styling fixes; skin-specific advice (pigmentation, marks); explicit realism statements.
- Intake should include ancestry (multi-select), aesthetic reference faces/preferences and hair characteristics, plus front/45°/90° photos.
- Can beat: projection quality (identity/age preserved, measured realism check), transparency about who reviews (named, credentialed reviewers), price/value perception, turnaround.
