# Marker catalogue: skin, eye appearance, global perception constructs

Compiled 2026-11-09 for the Gulf (UAE/KSA) launch of the face/body analysis product. Scope: skin markers that can be measured from a standard photo, eye-appearance markers, and global perception constructs. This sits alongside the Qoves benchmark (169 assessments, incl. Skin 20 and General 8).

## Intro

**How it was compiled.** Citations were checked against PubMed/Europe PMC records (REST API) or Crossref. Every DOI, volume and page below comes from those records. Most claims come from **abstracts only**: most of the papers are paywalled and no full text was read, except where a PMC open-access version was available (Del Bino 2018 for the ITA categories). "Perception evidence" only reports what the abstract or record says. "not found" means no published value turned up in what we could access. It does not mean no value exists.

**Count.** 56 markers: 26 skin, 10 eye appearance, 20 global construct.

**Capture requirements (our synthesis, not one source).**
- **Colour-dependent markers need a colour reference card in frame** (e.g. a 24-patch ColorChecker-type target) plus a fixed white balance. These are all CIELAB/ITA markers (skin colour, evenness, redness, yellowness, luminance, spots, PIH, melasma, periorbital darkness), plus sclera colour, iris colour and facial contrast. Without the card, phone auto white balance and tone mapping cancel out between-person and between-session differences. The perception studies these markers rest on used colour-calibrated or "carefully controlled" photographs (Stephen 2009a; Russell 2009; Russell 2014). Recommended conditions:
  - diffuse, frontal, even lighting (ring light or window light, no direct sun, no mixed colour temperatures)
  - no makeup (Russell 2009 shows cosmetics change facial contrast)
  - no filters or "beauty mode"
  - neutral expression, eyes open, looking at the lens
  - about 0.5–1 m away with no wide-angle distortion, hair off the forehead
- **Cross-polarised lighting** separates subsurface colour (pigment, haemoglobin) from surface glare. It is preferred for pigmentation and redness. **Parallel-polarised or specular capture** is needed for gloss/shine and helps with texture and pores (Matsubara 2012 split surface vs subsurface reflection). Consumer phones cannot polarise. So for in-app capture, gloss, pores and texture should be flagged as low-confidence or done with a clip-on accessory.
- **Several views and expressions.** The validated wrinkle and volume scales were built on standardised photos:
  - frontal plus 45° obliques for cheek fullness: the mid-face scales were more reliable with three views (Carruthers 2012)
  - dynamic expressions (frown, smile/squint, brow raise) for the dynamic glabellar, crow's feet and forehead grades (Flynn 2012)
- **Head covering (Gulf-specific).** Hijab, shayla or ghutra can hide the forehead, hairline, temples and jaw outline. This affects FWHR, face shape, forehead lines and averageness/symmetry landmarking. In Emirati raters, how much the hijab covered changed attractiveness ratings (Sheen 2018; Jordan 2020). Capture instructions should allow partial covering and still confirm the forehead and jawline are visible.

**Gulf and skin-of-colour evidence found**
- Middle-East acne PIH survey: PIH in 87.2% of 262 acne patients (Abanmi 2019).
- Jeddah cohort, n = 417: acne scarring alone 22.8%, PIH alone 22.3%, both 36.2% (Alamri 2025).
- Facial pigmentary demarcation lines in 14% of 1,033 Saudi women screened at dermatology clinics (Al-Samary 2010).
- Hail Region skin disease pattern (Parthasaradhi 1998). The melasma figure of 2.9% often attributed to it came from a secondary review only; the original abstract and full text were not accessible.
- Middle East groups made up 12.2% of the Farkas 2005 international facial anthropometry sample.
- Emirati attractiveness and hijab studies (Sheen 2018; Jordan 2020).
- Turkey was among the 10 rater cultures in Kočnar 2019.
- Skin-of-colour acne and PIH reviews (Taylor 2002; Davis & Callender 2010). Global melasma survey (Ortonne 2009): 90% of patients with a family history of melasma were skin types III–VI.

**Gaps**
- No periorbital hyperpigmentation prevalence or classification study in an Arab or Gulf population was found; the available ones are Singapore (Ranu 2011), Brazil (Matsui 2015) and Korea (Park 2016).
- No Gulf population-based melasma prevalence figure was found.
- No Skin Aging Atlas volume or validated wrinkle atlas built on Middle Eastern faces was found. The published Bazin/Flament volumes cover Caucasian, Asian, Afro-American and Indian types. Flament 2026 covers East Asian, African and European ancestry only.
- The Allergan/Merz validation samples are not described by ethnicity in their abstracts.
- Most perception studies (sclera, contrast, homogeneity, perceived age) used Caucasian female faces. Carotenoid-colour effects are framed in the literature mainly for lighter-skinned Asian and Caucasian faces (Whitehead 2012).
- No validated photonumeric scale for **under-eye bags** (lower-eyelid fat prolapse) or for **facial gloss** was found.
- No validated, consensus **face-shape classification** (oval/round/square, etc.) from 2D photos was found in the peer-reviewed literature.

Key: **auto** = computable from pixels or landmarks with no human judgement. **semi-auto** = algorithmic, but needs calibration, a human check, or a model trained on graded images. **reviewer grade** = a trained grader using a validated scale. Abbreviations: CCC = colour checker card; XPL = cross-polarised lighting.
